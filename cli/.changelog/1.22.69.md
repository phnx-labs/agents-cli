- **`--device auto` called an entire healthy fleet "unreachable" because the probe budget was shorter than a cold relayed SSH handshake (PHNX-3682).** Placement probed every candidate under one flat `PROBE_TIMEOUT_MS = 2_500`, whose docstring claimed to be "long enough for a cold relayed SSH handshake". It is not. On a fleet with no direct Tailscale paths (every peer `active; relay "sfo"`, which `tailscale netcheck` attributes to `MappingVariesByDestIP: true` / symmetric NAT), a cold relayed probe measured 1.7-6.6s across nine boxes probed in parallel — six of nine over budget, every one healthy and answering inside 10s. `agents run codex --device auto` then died with `no healthy device can run codex — excluded: … (unreachable)` for the whole pool, while `agents devices` showed the same boxes online at 1-6% load (it serves the 3-minute `.fleet-stats.json` cache; the resolver always probes live). The budget is now path-aware: a peer whose last handshake was relayed (`tailscale.direct === false`) gets `RELAYED_PROBE_TIMEOUT_MS = 8_000`, matching the readiness probe, while a direct or unknown-path peer keeps the tight 2.5s so a direct fleet stays fast — and a `via:"manual"` device, which never gets a tailscale peer entry, counts as unknown rather than silently widening. **A probe killed for exceeding its budget now reports as `probe timed out`, not `unreachable`**, in both `--device auto` and `agents devices pick`, and the error drops the misleading trailing "earliest window resets unknown" (which implied a rate limit) in favour of naming the latency and pointing at `tailscale status`. A device the probe returned no signal for at all now reads `no probe signal` instead of being folded into `unreachable`.
